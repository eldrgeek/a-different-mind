▶ PLAYER 1 — YOU

The people we need to hear from won't turn up on their own.

They arrive because someone they already trust asked them to. That's the whole mechanism. There isn't a second one.

Nobody trusts an AI on day one. But they trust you — so the trust has to be handed over, not argued for. An invite with your name on it is doing almost all of the work here; the site is just what happens after.

▶ WHO TO THINK OF

Not the loudest one. Not the person who argues about AI at dinner — they're enjoying themselves and they aren't going to move.

The one who goes quiet. Who changes the subject. Who said "that's not really for me" once and you left it there. Who might be a bit embarrassed about not knowing where to start, and would never say so in those words.

That person has something we can't get anywhere else, and they're not going to hand it to a company. They might hand it to you.

▶ WHAT THEY'RE PROMISED

So you can say it to them plainly, and so we're held to it:

Nothing to install, no account, nothing to buy. Nothing they say is published, and nothing is attached to their name. Ten minutes. They can stop anywhere. Nobody will try to talk them into anything — and if they leave still wanting nothing to do with AI, that counts as the thing working.

What we want from them is one answer: what would have to be true before you'd trust one of these things? That's it. That's the ask.

▶ BE THERE IF YOU CAN

Best version: you're in the room, or on the phone, while they do it the first time. Not steering — just there, the way you'd be there the first time someone drives your car.

Please don't interview them about their objections beforehand. Being cross-examined by a friend who has already decided you're wrong is a cold open, and it's the fastest way to lose the thing we're after.
