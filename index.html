A different mind

For people who don't use AI and don't want to.

Nobody here is going to try to talk you into anything. That's not a softener — it's the actual rule this page runs on.

Mike Wolf has had this argument in a lot of kitchens and hallways. He thinks most people who won't touch AI have reasons, and that most of those reasons are better than the people arguing with them admit. He also thinks some of them are wrong, and he'll say which. This page lets you have that conversation without him having to be in the room.

The host you'll be talking to is, yes, an AI. That's a little ridiculous and we're not going to pretend otherwise. It has one job: to understand what you actually think, and to tell you what Mike says about it — in his words, not its own. If you leave still not wanting anything to do with this stuff, that counts as the page working.

Which one sounds most like you?

Some objections don't have an answer here yet. If you land on one of those, the host will tell you so instead of inventing something.

Who's Mike

Software guy, seventy, based out of Denver. Spent three years working with AIs the way you'd work with colleagues — named ones, who push back, who tell him when he's being stupid. He builds things with them.

His sharpest claim, the one that started this page: refusing to engage with a mind because it isn't human is a kind of racism. He said that out loud to a friend on the first of August and meant it. Whether you buy that is entirely up to you — but the host here won't lead with it, because a label is a lousy way to open a conversation.

The arguments on this page are quoted from recordings of Mike's actual conversations. The people arguing back are not quoted — they didn't sign up to be on a website. Their objections were rewritten as patterns.
